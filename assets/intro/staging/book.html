Chapter Three

The Harbour Wall

By the time Mara reached the harbour wall, the sun had slipped behind the headland, and the last gleam of it lay on the water like a thread pulled loose from a coat. She sat on the cold stones and waited. Below her, the boats knocked against one another in the swell, patient as old dogs.

Her father had told her once that the sea kept every promise made beside it, though never in the way you meant. She had not believed him then. She was not sure she believed him now, but she had come anyway, and that, she supposed, was a kind of answer.

A light came on in the window of the customs house. Somebody was still awake down there, bent over a ledger or a letter, and the thought of it made the evening seem a little less enormous.
